Privacy Policy

Tidel is a motocross lap timer: it records where you ride, to tell you how fast you rode. This page says what that means for your data.

Last updated 16 November 2026 · applies to Tidel for iOS and Android · Norwegian version: personvern

The short version: your sessions, with track and heart rate, are private by default. Three things still become visible to others: your best lap time on the track’s list with your name (section 5), a new track you ride, with your name as a credit (section 6), and your riding on the track itself, without name or account, in the shared model of the track (section 7). You turn the best lap off under More → Privacy, where you also opt out of the track model. Usage statistics are on from the start and turned off under More → Feedback (section 2). No advertising, and no analytics tools from other companies in the app (section 5). The account and the sessions live on Tidel’s server; section 9 says what is deleted with the account, and what is not.

Position

Position is used while recording and becomes the track: on Android in a foreground service with a fixed notification, on iPhone “while using the app”, also with the screen locked. The driving route in Explore sends your position rounded to 0.02° and the track’s position to openrouteservice when the service is on.

Your numbers and settings

Age, weight, resting heart rate and your settings are stored with the account while Cloud sync is on (it is until you turn it off), so you get them back on a new phone. The device register (RaceBox and heart-rate belt, with serial number) follows the settings; from Tidel 1.21 the session carries only model, firmware, data rate and mounting, and the RaceBox fingerprint with the serial number stays on the phone.

Usage statistics and feedback

Each install gets a random installation id, which sits on usage statistics, sessions, tracks you add and feedback. Usage statistics are fixed event names with small numbers, app version, platform, language and time, with no account id, and never lap times, position or names. They are on from the start, and turned off under More → Feedback (“Usage stats”) or in the welcome. The app’s own error reports (text, file name and line) are sent with the same switch. “What stopped you?” is asked once, only after Tidel opens to everyone, after seven days without a session of your own, never while recording; the answer is sent as usage statistics. Feedback you send yourself under More → Feedback, with optional contact address, attachments you choose, app version, installation id, and from Tidel 1.21 the account id when signed in. The owner gets the day’s numbers and new feedback by email, with the text and the contact address you wrote; Resend sends the email, and it is not deleted with the account.

What Tidel does not do

  • No advertising id and no hardware id from the phone; the app makes the installation id itself.
  • No public leaderboard other than the track’s own, which you can turn off (section 5).

3. What stays off Tidel’s server

  • The backup in the app’s folder has everything on the phone: sessions with track and heart rate, settings, the device register with serial number, friend pictures and the account id, never the sign-in. On iPhone the folder follows the iCloud backup when on (Apple’s default); turn Tidel off there to keep your heart rate out. On Android, Google’s automatic backup is off for Tidel. Exporting one session (.mxsession) leaves out firmware and Health id, and from Tidel 1.21 the RaceBox serial number.
  • A picture you set on a friend lives only on the phone and in the backup.
  • Map tiles you have viewed (up to 30 days, so the map shows without coverage) and sector passes from practice stay only on the phone.
  • The camera reads QR codes in memory; nothing is stored or sent.

4. Your account

The account is a random id and an 8-character share code, plus platform and app version; it owns your sessions. The share code is seen only by whoever you show or send it to; your friends see the id. In the beta the account is anonymous until you attach an email address; when Tidel opens to everyone, it needs an email and a password. The password is stored only as a hash at Supabase, and your friends never see the address. When an email is attached or changed, a password set or the account linked to the web version, the time, the address and a short code of the IP address (reversible for IPv4) are stored.

The rider name is required and may be a nickname; the class is optional, and your friends see both. The name follows your best lap to the track’s list and is added as a credit on tracks you add, both on by default (sections 5 and 6). Today the name must be unused across Tidel, checked against the server before the account exists; the server update that drops that is written but not live.

“Where did you hear about Tidel?” comes with sign-up: one of seven fixed answers, stored as a code on the account. “Send me news from Tidel” stays unticked until you tick it yourself; we store when you said yes, which text, and when you withdrew it under More → Privacy. No such email has been sent yet. If you tap “Later” when the app asks for an email, the server counts only how many times. If you buy Tidel Pro, we store whether the account has Pro, source, store, until when, renewal, and when it was bought, paid and stopped; the card stays with Apple or Google. RevenueCat, which handles the purchase, gets the account’s id. If you delete the account, what we store about Tidel Pro is deleted, not the purchase at RevenueCat and the store; the subscription runs until you cancel it in the store. Lifetime is a one-off purchase, with no subscription to cancel. If you delete the account, the profile and the sign-in are deleted; section 9 says when.

5. Sharing, and who else processes data

What you share yourself

A session is private until you turn on “Share this session with friends” for that session. The GPS track is a private file only your account, and friends you share the session with, can open. If you share, your friends get the whole session: laps, times, the GPS track, heart rate, the device’s model and firmware, the installation id and the source name from Health (often the watch’s). From Tidel 1.21 sessions go without the RaceBox serial number; sessions uploaded with an older version have it in the cloud, also for the friends you share with, until the clean-up is run. Your friend’s phone keeps a copy until their app next syncs after you stop sharing or delete the session or the account; a file they have exported stays with them. If you share a lap as a link or QR code (tidel.no/r/), the times, the track and the line sit in the link itself after #, which no server sees; your name is never in it. The buddy link tidel.no/v/ carries your share code and your name after #; tidel.no only sees that the page was opened. As soon as you are friends, the friend sees name, class, picture, home track, when you last opened the app, your records with the line and your track days with notes. “Share my records” off keeps the records from your friends too. The profile picture goes to your friends at 128 pixels as soon as you set it; 256 pixels are stored with the settings on the account. Reactions and track days you enter yourself, and your friends see them until you delete them.

Your best lap on the track’s list

Your best lap on a track is shared on the track’s list by default: time, date, your name or initial, the line of that lap and whether the finish line was approved. The list is open to everyone, also without an account. You turn it off for one session while reviewing it (“Share my best lap on the track’s list”) or for good under More → Privacy (“Share my records”); a time already there is removed. “Show full name on lists” in the same place chooses full name or initial. Heart rate, the whole session and position outside the lap are never shared on the list.

Training sessions

In a training session the coach sees your name, group and attendance, and with phone recording for a heat also your lap times from it with sector times, speed and data quality. The coach never sees your GPS track, your heart rate or other sessions. If you choose “Phone + map line”, a simplified map line of your best lap in the heat is shared with the coach and the others in the session, never anyone outside it. The group result list is off by default; when it is on, participants in the group see each other’s names, lap times and sector times. You can leave, or withdraw a result or a map line, at any time, and “Participate without recording” is always a full option. A watch shares exactly the same, and you always start it yourself.

Watches and Health

From Apple Health or Health Connect, Tidel gets the workout with route, heart rate, distance, the source name (often the watch’s) and the Health id, stored as an ordinary session. If you connect a Garmin watch, we store the watch’s id, model, pairing code and the sessions it sends; Garmin gets nothing. If you connect Polar, Polar gets the account’s id, and Tidel stores the Polar user id and the access (server side only); if you disconnect, Tidel asks Polar to delete the link.

6. The shared track catalogue

The catalogue holds each track’s name, position, country, municipality and club, outline, finish line and sector boundaries. A known registered track goes there, credited to your name, the first time you upload or review a session there, set a line, save a layout or rename it, sync on or off; from Tidel 1.21 a new place goes only once you save the “New place” sheet with a name and “Share this place with everyone” on (older versions send it when the track is created, unless the switch is off); neither if you last saved it off. The outline may be the line of your best lap, at most 120 points. Unless hidden, others see a registered track at once, another after three sessions on two days; anyone can read the credit, even without an account. The finish line you set, line proposals, the layout you ride and track info you write are sent with the session and shown to everyone; the proposals and who wrote them are seen only by Tidel. If you delete the account, the track, line and info stay, without your name and account id where stored with a key; the track’s installation id and the line proposals’ pseudonym remain.

7. The track model: ground, jumps and lines

The ground, the jumps and the ruts are the same for everyone, so Tidel builds one shared model of each track from everyone’s riding. What is used is the part of your sessions that lies on the track itself while you ride: position, height, speed, heading, g-forces and accuracy, only on shared tracks where at least three riders have ridden. Before anything is used, your account is removed (replaced by a code per track that only the server’s key can trace back), along with your name, heart rate, time of day, device and everything outside the track itself.

The finished model is public: the ground and the lines as an average of many passes, only where at least three riders have ridden, and for a jump with at least three riders the median length, airtime and approach speed, the number of passes and riders, and the density in coarse levels. Under each jump, other riders’ apps get the ground profile from each pass, which shows where that pass took off and landed and the line there, given only to signed-in riders where at least three other riders with the contribution on have profiles within 8 m, without name, account, date or time of day, with new codes for every lookup. From Tidel 1.21 the app sends ground profiles only from your own recordings; a friend with an older app may send some from a session you share; those, and profiles from accounts deleted before 16 November 2026, are cleaned up by a server update not yet live. The extracts before combination are pseudonymised, used only by Tidel through a locked, logged analysis access, and shared with nobody.

The model shows where the track’s jumps and bumps are, and the number each jump has, counted from the finish line. For each place only its position, its direction and whether it is a jump or a bump are published, and for a jump whether the take-offs are tightly grouped. Speed, length, landing and how many have ridden there are published only for jumps with at least three riders, and how often anyone stays on the ground is never published. A place is included once at least three passes have been airborne there. Each jump may get a jump type (tabletop, double, step-up) and a landing zone. These are worked out from the terrain alone, the laser scan and the parts of the model where at least three riders have ridden, never from where anyone landed. Tracks without enough riding may get a 3D model made only from the Norwegian Mapping Authority’s laser scan, with the satellite image on top; it has no riding from anyone and says which year the scan is from.

You can opt out: turn off “Contribute to the track model” under More → Privacy, and none of your sessions are used, old ones included, while you still see the model. The switch is on from the start, and exists from Tidel 1.17; with an older version, update the app or write to post@tidel.no with the code under More → My share code. If you delete the account, your ground profiles are deleted with it, and a deleted account never contributes to the track model. What has already been folded into a finished model is an average with no link to you, and stays. The terrain comes from the Norwegian Mapping Authority’s open elevation data, and the background is the track map’s satellite image (Esri, section 5).

8. How the data is kept

  • Everything sent to or from the server travels over HTTPS.
  • Track files live in a private storage bucket, bound to the account that owns them, with no public link; the exception is the locked analysis access in section 7.
  • The database itself enforces access per row: your sessions are readable by your account, and by friends only when you share.
  • Usage statistics can be read by no app or rider, only by Tidel through the database.

9. Retention and deletion

Sessions are kept until you delete them; a deleted session is marked deleted with its laps and record; the row is not cleaned away. From Tidel 1.21 the app deletes the track file of a deleted session; a file it did not delete is unreadable to everyone and is removed by the nightly job.

Deleting the whole account

You start it under More → Your account → Delete my account, or from tidel.no/delete-account without the app. The account is locked at once, and you have a 7-day grace period. From Tidel 1.21 the phone then also sends its installation id, so the usage statistics from it are deleted with the account. The night after the grace period a nightly job completes the deletion, even if the app is uninstalled; you can also complete it with the button “Complete deletion now” in the app. Usage statistics, error reports and the answer to “What stopped you?” carry no account id, only the installation id; when the account is deleted, they are deleted for the phones we know are yours. Those are the phones that uploaded your sessions and tracks or sent feedback with the account, and from Tidel 1.21 the phone you delete from; if another account has also used the phone, the statistics from it stay. Feedback is deleted with the account when it was sent with it (from Tidel 1.21), and otherwise when it came from one of the same phones; from a phone another account has also used, it is kept with the contact address. When the account is deleted, a receipt with a salted pseudonym of the account id and counts is written; it has no id and remains.

How long it is kept

Usage statistics are not cleaned up today; the nightly job that deletes them after 90 days is switched off. Training sessions are not cleaned up today; how long a session and what is shared in it stays when nobody withdraws has not yet been decided. If you leave, or withdraw a result or a map line, it disappears at once at the coach’s end; if you delete the account, heat tracks, results and your participation are deleted, and training sessions you own are marked deleted. Expired pairing codes for Garmin and Polar are deleted at the next pairing, not on a timer; the link is deleted when you disconnect or delete the account. The log of lookups in the analysis access is deleted after 180 days; deleted sessions are not included. The daily caps against abuse count calls per account, per installation id and per short code of the IP address, and the counters are cleared after two days. The waiting list on tidel.no is tied to no account; unconfirmed addresses are cleaned up after 30 days and unsubscribed ones after 90 (unsubscribe at tidel.no/avmeld/). The driving-route cache and quotas have no clean-up yet; the proposal is 90 and 2 days. Feedback is kept with no time limit, until it is deleted with the account or you ask us to remove it. What you delete stays in Supabase’s daily backups of the database until they expire.

10. Your rights

Under the GDPR you may request access, correction, deletion, restriction, and a portable copy. Two of these you do yourself: More → Backup exports everything, and section 9 deletes the account. For the rest, write to post@tidel.no.

The basis is the service you asked for, and consent for the optional, such as Apple Health or Health Connect access, withdrawn in your phone’s settings. The track model (section 7) and usage statistics (section 2) rest on legitimate interest (Article 6(1)(f)): correct measurements of a track for everyone, and faults that get found; the model gets pseudonymised data cut to the track, and the statistics carry no account id. You can object: turn off “Contribute to the track model” under More → Privacy and “Usage stats” under More → Feedback.

We disclose data to a public authority only where the law requires it. If you believe your data is handled wrongly, complain to the Norwegian Data Protection Authority (Datatilsynet).

11. Children

Motocross riders are often young. The app does not ask for a date of birth, does not profile users, shows no advertising, and has no public social feed. It is not directed at children under 13; a guardian can have an account removed with one email to post@tidel.no.

One thing a parent should know: the best lap time on a track is shared on that track’s list by default, along with the name entered in the app (section 5). It is turned off while reviewing a session or under More → Privacy, where you also choose full name or initial.

Training sessions are for adult participants during the pilot phase; guardian consent must be in place first, and the app will say so when that changes.

12. Changes

When this policy changes, the date at the top changes; the app can announce a change only in a new app version.
